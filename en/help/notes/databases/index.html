Review note

Databases

In databases, getting the right answer matters as much as obtaining it consistently and efficiently. The fundamentals are indexes, transactions, joins, cardinality, business keys, and auditing. Explaining these concepts helps support backend and data decisions.

Indexes

An index speeds up reads when it matches frequent filters, joins, or orderings. The trade-off is that every write must keep those indexes up to date. An index is not automatically good: it depends on the query, volume, selectivity, and change frequency.

Over-indexing

Creating indexes “just in case” can slow operations such as INSERT , UPDATE , DELETE , and MERGE . Too many indexes can make writes slower, consume more space, and produce less clear plans. The right approach is to inspect real queries, execution plans, and index usage.

Transactions

A transaction groups changes that must be confirmed together. If one part fails, a rollback can prevent partial data. This matters in loads, reconciliations, state changes, and operations where several tables represent a single business action.

Traceability

Traceability answers what happened, when it happened, what input was used, and what result was produced. Fields such as created_at , updated_at , user, status, checksum, batch_id, or job_id help audit processes and diagnose issues.

Typical questions

  • How would you explain a clustered versus nonclustered index at a high level?
  • Why does an index not always improve a query?
  • When should a transaction be used?
  • Which columns would you include for operational traceability?
