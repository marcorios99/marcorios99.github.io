Review note

OCR, images and documents

Document processing combines images, extraction, and validation. OCR reads text, ICR attempts to recognise handwriting or more variable fields, and OMR detects marks. Before extracting data, it is often necessary to improve the image and crop regions of interest.

OCR, ICR and OMR

OCR focuses on recognising printed or scanned text. ICR targets handwriting or highly variable fields. OMR detects marks, checkboxes, or selections. A real form can use all three techniques: marks for answers, printed text for labels, and handwritten fields for identification.

Preprocessing

Before information is extracted, an image may need grayscale conversion, binarisation, noise reduction, contrast or perspective correction. The aim is to help the algorithm see the relevant structure instead of shadows, backgrounds, stains, or irregular scans.

Contours and ROIs

Contours help detect candidate regions: checkboxes, tables, text blocks, or document edges. An ROI is a region of interest cropped to process a specific field. Defining good ROIs reduces false positives and makes the pipeline easier to explain.

Validation and evidence

Extraction does not end when OCR returns text. Formats, ranges, required fields, confidence, and business-rule consistency must be validated. Keeping evidence such as the original image, processed ROI, checksum, and structured result helps audit and correct errors.
